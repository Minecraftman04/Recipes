Spanish-Style Padrón Peppers

Pimientos de Padrón: smoky, blistered little green peppers, soft in the middle and generously finished with flaky sea salt. The traditional three-ingredient version is hard to beat.

Serves
2–3 as tapas
Prep time
3 mins
Cook time
4–6 mins
Total time
7–10 mins
Equipment
Large heavy frying pan and tongs
Watch out: hot oil can spit when the peppers go in. Dry them thoroughly, add them carefully and avoid crowding the pan. The occasional Padrón pepper can also be surprisingly spicy.

Ingredients

  • 250 g fresh Padrón peppers, left whole with stems attached
  • 1½ tbsp (about 22 ml) olive oil, preferably good-quality extra virgin
  • ½ tsp flaky sea salt (such as Maldon), or to taste

Method

  1. Wash and dry. Rinse the peppers, then pat them completely dry with kitchen paper or a clean tea towel. Leave them whole; there is no need to deseed them.
  2. Get the pan hot. Heat a large, heavy frying pan over medium-high to high heat. Add the olive oil and let it get hot and shimmering, but do not let it smoke heavily.
  3. Blister the first side. Carefully add the peppers in one layer. They should sizzle straight away. Leave them mostly undisturbed for about 1–2 minutes to form dark blisters.
  4. Turn and finish. Toss or turn with tongs, then fry for another 2–4 minutes, turning occasionally, until blistered on several sides and softened but not blackened all over. Cook in batches if the pan is crowded.
  5. Salt immediately. Tip the peppers onto a serving plate, sprinkle over the flaky sea salt and toss lightly so it sticks to the warm, oily skins.
  6. Eat straight away. Serve piping hot as tapas. Hold each pepper by the stem, eat the soft pepper and discard the stem.

Secrets to the best Padrón peppers

  • Dry peppers = better blistering. Water makes the pan steam and can cause the oil to spit.
  • Give them space. A single layer gets the quick smoky char that makes this dish special.
  • Look for blisters, not charcoal. Brown and black patches are ideal; the flesh should remain tender rather than shrivelled or bitter.
  • Salt at the end. Flaky sea salt adds bursts of crunch and flavour without drawing moisture out during frying.
  • Keep it simple. No garlic, lemon or sauce is needed for the classic Spanish tapas-bar flavour.

Air-fryer alternative

Toss the dry peppers with the olive oil, spread in a single layer and air-fry at 200°C for approximately 6–8 minutes, shaking halfway through. Finish with flaky salt. This is convenient, but a very hot frying pan gives the most traditional texture.
