Quick Bolognese with Red Wine

Rich, savoury and meat-forward, with a deeper flavour without an hours-long simmer.

Serves
4
Total time
40–45 mins
Active time
20 mins
Style
Meat-forward
Prefer no wine? Replace it with 60 ml stock and an optional splash of balsamic vinegar.

Ingredients

For the base

  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely diced
  • 1 large carrot, finely diced or grated
  • 1 celery stalk, finely diced (optional)
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 500 g beef mince (ideally 10–15% fat), or beef/pork mix

For the sauce

  • 2 tbsp tomato purée
  • 120 ml dry red wine
  • 1 × 400 g tin chopped tomatoes
  • 1 tsp dried oregano or Italian mixed herbs
  • 60–120 ml beef stock or water, only as needed
  • 80–120 ml whole milk (optional)
  • Salt and freshly ground black pepper

To serve

  • 350–400 g spaghetti or tagliatelle
  • Freshly grated Parmesan
  • A little reserved pasta water

Method

  1. Soften the vegetables. Heat the oil in a wide, heavy pan over medium heat. Add onion, carrot and optional celery with a pinch of salt. Cook for 6–8 minutes, stirring, until soft. Add garlic for the final 30 seconds.
  2. Brown the mince. Turn the heat up to medium-high. Add mince and break it up, then let it develop some browned patches. Cook for 7–10 minutes until the excess moisture has evaporated. Season lightly with pepper.
  3. Cook the tomato purée. Stir in tomato purée and cook for 1–2 minutes, stirring, until it darkens slightly and smells sweeter. Do this before adding liquid.
  4. Reduce the wine. Pour in red wine, scraping the browned bits from the base of the pan. Simmer briskly for 3–5 minutes, until reduced by roughly half.
  5. Simmer the sauce. Add chopped tomatoes and herbs. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cook uncovered for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add small splashes of stock or water only if it becomes too thick.
  6. Finish gently. If using milk, stir it in for the last 5 minutes over low heat. Taste and adjust salt and pepper. The finished sauce should be rich, not watery.
  7. Marry pasta and sauce. Meanwhile, boil pasta in well-salted water until just short of al dente. Reserve a mug of pasta water before draining. Toss pasta in the sauce for 1–2 minutes, adding a little pasta water until glossy. Serve with Parmesan.

Make it even better

  • Brown, do not steam: give the meat space and let moisture evaporate before stirring repeatedly.
  • Control the liquid: only add stock if the sauce looks dry; it should be thick enough to cling to pasta.
  • Prefer no wine? Replace it with 60 ml stock and a splash of balsamic vinegar (optional).
